Section A: Multiple Choice Questions (40 marks)
1.
Which of the following is the largest organ in the human body?
[2 marks]
A. Liver
B. Lungs
C. Skin
D. Brain
Answer
C. Skin. The skin (integumentary system) is the largest organ by surface area and weight, covering approximately 1.5 to 2 square metres in adults and accounting for roughly 15% of total body weight.
2.
The mitral valve is located between which two chambers of the heart?
[2 marks]
A. Right atrium and right ventricle
B. Left atrium and left ventricle
C. Left ventricle and aorta
D. Right ventricle and pulmonary artery
Answer
B. Left atrium and left ventricle. The mitral (bicuspid) valve prevents backflow of blood from the left ventricle into the left atrium during ventricular systole. It has two leaflets, distinguishing it from the tricuspid valve on the right side.
3.
Which bone forms the posterior part of the hard palate?
[2 marks]
A. Maxilla
B. Vomer
C. Palatine bone
D. Sphenoid
Answer
C. Palatine bone. The hard palate is formed anteriorly by the palatine processes of the maxillae and posteriorly by the horizontal plates of the palatine bones.
Section B: Structured Questions (60 marks)
4.
Describe the gross anatomy of the liver. Include its lobes, surfaces, ligaments, and its relationship to adjacent organs. [15 marks]
Model answer
Location and surfaces: The liver is located in the right hypochondriac and epigastric regions, beneath the diaphragm. It has a diaphragmatic surface (smooth and convex) and a visceral surface (irregular, faces inferiorly and posteriorly).

Lobes: The right lobe is the largest. The left lobe is separated from the right by the falciform ligament. The caudate and quadrate lobes are visible on the visceral surface, separated by the porta hepatis, the fossa for the gallbladder, and the fossa for the inferior vena cava.

Ligaments: The falciform ligament attaches the liver to the anterior abdominal wall. The round ligament (ligamentum teres) runs in its free edge. The coronary ligament attaches the liver to the diaphragm. The right and left triangular ligaments are reflections of the coronary ligament.

Relations: Superiorly, the diaphragm. Inferiorly and posteriorly, the stomach, duodenum, right kidney, right adrenal gland, hepatic flexure of colon, and gallbladder.
5.
With the aid of a diagram, describe the brachial plexus. Include its roots, trunks, divisions, cords, and major terminal branches. [15 marks]
Model answer
Roots: C5, C6, C7, C8, and T1 (ventral rami).

Trunks: C5 and C6 form the upper trunk. C7 continues as the middle trunk. C8 and T1 form the lower trunk.

Divisions: Each trunk divides into anterior and posterior divisions, giving 6 divisions total.

Cords: The lateral cord is formed by the anterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks. The medial cord is the continuation of the anterior division of the lower trunk. The posterior cord is formed by all three posterior divisions.

Major terminal branches: Musculocutaneous nerve (lateral cord, C5 to C7). Median nerve (lateral and medial cords, C5 to T1). Ulnar nerve (medial cord, C8 to T1). Radial nerve (posterior cord, C5 to T1). Axillary nerve (posterior cord, C5 to C6).

A well-labelled diagram showing the above organisation would earn up to 5 of the 15 marks.
6.
Describe the peritoneum and its clinical significance. Distinguish between intraperitoneal and retroperitoneal organs and give two examples of each. [15 marks]
Model answer
Peritoneum: A thin serous membrane lining the abdominal cavity (parietal peritoneum) and covering the abdominal organs (visceral peritoneum). The potential space between them is the peritoneal cavity, which contains a small amount of serous fluid that reduces friction.

Clinical significance: Peritonitis results from inflammation, often due to bowel perforation or infection. Ascites is abnormal fluid accumulation in the peritoneal cavity. The peritoneum also forms mesenteries, omenta, and ligaments that carry vessels and nerves to organs.

Intraperitoneal organs (covered by visceral peritoneum on all sides, suspended by mesentery): stomach, jejunum, ileum, transverse colon, sigmoid colon, spleen, liver.

Retroperitoneal organs (behind the parietal peritoneum): kidneys, ureters, aorta, inferior vena cava, pancreas (body and tail partially), duodenum (second to fourth parts), ascending and descending colon.
